\begin{abstract}
A logistic fit's precision is set by the number of positive cases rather than by
the sample size. This report asks whether the same threshold governs a
generator. CART-based synthesis (\texttt{synthpop}) is run on MIMIC-IV
($n = 70{,}954$ ICU stays) under two sweeps that hold the generator fixed: one
raises the rare stratum's positive count $k$ at fixed sample size, the other
raises the sample size $n$ at fixed $k$. Together they separate count from
prevalence.

The threshold does carry over, but the generator adds none of its own. Raising
$k$ drives divergence to zero and sharply narrows the spread of the estimates,
while a matched increase in $n$ moves neither. The real arm's divergence
rate and a logistic fit to the same subsamples never involve the synthesizer, so
the threshold belongs to the stratum and \texttt{synthpop} inherits rather than
imposes it. Synthesis adds a bias of its own that no count
removes: in a companion simulation with a known truth, the synthetic median
recovers 99\% of an interaction of 0.5 but only 41\% of one of 3. The finding
transfers as a mechanism and not as a remedy.

A 500-row subsample cannot resolve the interactions MIMIC-IV offers: the
smallest one it could detect is seven to twelve times the one present. Two
recommendations follow: establish that a target effect is estimable
in the source data before attributing any discrepancy to the generator, and
calibrate variable-selection diagnostics against a permutation null, which here
moves from 0.00 to 0.32 with class balance alone.
\end{abstract}

% -------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}

That a logistic fit's precision is set by the event count is established
\cite{peduzzi1996, vansmeden2016}, and separation at thin counts has been
characterised since \cite{albert1984}. In earlier coursework I found the same
under Bayesian logistic regression on the \texttt{birthwt} dataset: posterior
stability depends on a predictor's positive-case count rather than on overall
sample size. Nobody has shown whether the same threshold governs a
\emph{generator}. Whether a CART-based synthesizer preserves a rare-stratum
interaction is a question about which variables a tree spends a split on, not
about the curvature of a likelihood, and the two need not share a threshold.

Synthetic data shares clinical records without exposing patients, and is usually
judged by general utility, how closely the synthetic joint distribution
resembles the real one \cite{snoke2018}. A generator can score well on that and
still lose the one coefficient a study was designed around, so what follows
holds the generator fixed and measures a single interaction rather than overall
similarity. It matters because of a specific misreading. A researcher handed a
synthetic release of MIMIC-IV finds the CHF by creatinine interaction absent
from it, and reasonably concludes that the synthesizer destroyed it. In the 500
rows in front of them that effect was never detectable to begin with, and the
same discrepancy would have appeared with no synthesizer involved at all.
Three questions follow, and the answers come from different places. Whether the
count threshold reaches a generator is settled by the two sweeps on MIMIC-IV.
Whether synthesis recovers the effect it was given cannot be settled there, so a
simulation with a known truth answers it instead. Whether a split diagnostic can be read at
all is a question about the audit rather than the generator, and closes the
Results. Code is available at
\href{https://github.com/ShengPeiWilliam/mimic-synthetic-fidelity}{GitHub Repository}.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\section{Data}

\subsection{Cohort}

The cohort is MIMIC-IV v2.2 \cite{johnson2023}, restricted to hospital
admissions with an associated ICU stay, one row per stay. Dropping the 3\% with
no first-day creatinine leaves a pool of 70{,}954, from which all subsampling
draws. That 3\% is not a random slice: a stay ending within hours has no time
for the lab draw, so the pool leans toward stays that lasted long enough to be
measured. The rows that remain repeat patients, so standard errors on the
full-pool estimate are clustered.

\subsection{Variables}

The outcome is in-hospital mortality. Three predictors enter every fit: age,
first-day maximum creatinine, and one comorbidity flag. Comorbidities are simplified ICD-9/ICD-10 prefix flags rather
than a validated index such as Elixhauser or Charlson, since the design needs a
known positive count. The three least prevalent of the five screened were
carried forward: COPD (11\%), CHF (28\%) and Diabetes (31\%). Even the rarest is
too common to supply a thin stratum, so it is manufactured by subsampling.
Creatinine is capped at the 99.5th percentile and left untransformed, so every
creatinine coefficient below is per mg/dL.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Experimental Design}

Both sweeps draw subsamples stratified on the comorbidity flag, $k$ rows
carrying it and $n - k$ without, and synthesize each with
\texttt{synthpop::syn()} \cite{nowok2016}. One model is fitted to the real
subsample and again to the synthetic table, giving paired estimates
$\hat\beta_{\text{real}}$ and $\hat\beta_{\text{syn}}$ of the interaction at
$M = 100$ Monte Carlo replicates per grid point:
\[
\texttt{hospital\_expire\_flag} \sim \texttt{age} + \texttt{creatinine}
  + \texttt{comorbidity}
  + \underbrace{\texttt{comorbidity}{:}\texttt{creatinine}}_{\hat\beta_{\text{real}},\ \hat\beta_{\text{syn}}}
\]

The distance between the two estimates is the fidelity measure, and the same
subsamples carry over to the tree diagnosis, so one draw supports both analyses
in Section~\ref{sec:analyses}. Both arms come from the same subsample, so the pool's
selection cancels in the comparison. Each sweep holds one input fixed and varies the other (Table~\ref{tab:design}).
The count sweep moves prevalence along with the count; the size sweep moves
prevalence alone, holding the count at 20, where the interaction draws its
information mostly from the positive stratum and rests on the deaths inside
twenty rows however large the subsample grows around them.

Sweep & Held fixed & Varied & What moves \\
\midrule
Count sweep    & $n = 500$ & $k \in \{5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 150, 250\}$
                  & count and prevalence together \\
Size sweep     & $k = 20$  & $n \in \{250, 500, 1000, 2000, 4000\}$
                  & prevalence only, 8\% to 0.5\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The two sweeps. Each holds the generator fixed and varies one input.}
\label{tab:design}
\end{table}

Throughout, a \emph{series} is one comorbidity in one arm, six in all, and a
\emph{cell} is one series at one grid point.

\subsection{Analyses}
\label{sec:analyses}

Two analyses run on every grid point. The first compares
$\hat\beta_{\text{real}}$ against $\hat\beta_{\text{syn}}$ on the median and
interquartile width of the converged estimates, and on how often a fit fails at
all (Section~\ref{sec:failure}). The synthetic arm is fitted the way a data
recipient would fit it: one synthetic copy, an ordinary GLM.
The second refits the same subsamples with \texttt{rpart} under
\texttt{synthpop::syn.cart}'s control parameters and records whether the
comorbidity earns a split; a likelihood-ratio test dropping the comorbidity and
its interaction asks a GLM the same question. Every configuration is re-run with
the comorbidity shuffled, which holds the positive count fixed but destroys its
link to the outcome, giving the null.

\subsection{The Full-Pool Reference}

MIMIC-IV carries no known ground truth, so the same model fitted on the whole
pool stands in as the reference, $\hat\beta_{\text{full}}$, and every subsample
estimate is read against it. All three are real, differ in sign, and run roughly
three to four times smaller than the creatinine slope they modify
(Table~\ref{tab:fullpool}).

\subsection{Two Failure Modes}
\label{sec:failure}

Fits at the smallest counts fail often enough that the rates are reported as a
result, not used as a filter. They fail in two ways. The first is
\textbf{inestimable}: the table holds no positives, so the interaction returns
\texttt{NA}. The second is \textbf{divergent}: the term is returned but runs
past $|2|$, forty times the full-pool estimate, marking a likelihood maximized
at the boundary \cite{heinze2002}.

Only the second appears in the real arm, which holds exactly $k$ positives by
construction, while the synthetic table generates its own column and can come
back empty. Rates are shares of all $M$ replicates, so converged, inestimable
and divergent sum to one, and every quantile below uses converged fits only.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{What This Design Can and Cannot Resolve}
\label{sec:scope}

The subject here is what a generator does to a small subsample, so $n$ is set by
the question rather than by what the data could support. Measuring what that
choice costs is what fixes the scale everything below is read on. At $n = 500$
the smallest interaction detectable at 80\% power runs seven to twelve times the
one the pool holds (Table~\ref{tab:floor}), and reaching these effects would
take most of the pool.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Comorbidity & Smallest $|\beta|$ detectable & Factor & Rows needed \\
\midrule
CHF      & 0.47 & 9.5$\times$  & 45{,}000 \\
Diabetes & 0.49 & 6.8$\times$  & 23{,}000 \\
COPD     & 0.78 & 11.6$\times$ & 67{,}000 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The detection floor at 80\% power. An interaction must reach the first
column before a 500-row subsample finds it four times in five; the factor
compares that threshold with $\hat\beta_{\text{full}}$ in
Table~\ref{tab:fullpool}.}
\label{tab:floor}
\end{table}

No subsample this size can say whether synthesis recovers a true interaction, so
the sweeps do not ask. They measure precision and failure behaviour, readable at
any effect size, and leave bias to a companion simulation where the truth is
known (Section~\ref{sec:sim}).

\section{Results}

\subsection{Count, Not Sample Size, Removes Divergence}

\paragraph{Count sweep: divergence stops by a count of eighty.}
Divergence runs at 18 to 40\% at $k = 5$ and every series is at zero by $k = 80$.
Not at one shared count: two are there already
at $k = 40$, and a zero over 100 replicates bounds a rate at 3.6\% rather than
proving it nil, so the threshold sits in $(40, 80]$ for each series. Neither
prevalence nor the sign of the interaction moves it.

\paragraph{Size sweep: divergence never stops.}
No sample size does the same. Over a matched eightfold increase in $n$ divergence
stays between 2 and 24\% and no series reaches zero. The arm separates cleanly
here and the size does not: the synthetic rate is higher in fourteen of the
fifteen comorbidity-by-size comparisons, and no sample size closes the gap.

\paragraph{Checking the $|2|$ rule.}
The $|2|$ threshold is one this study set, not one the data supplies, so it is
checked against a separation test. It understates divergence at the smallest
counts: a penalized fit \cite{firth1993} finds 64 to 76\% of real fits separated
at $k = 5$ where $|2|$ flags 18 to 34\%. The two agree from $k = 20$ up, and the
penalized fit does not move the count at which divergence stops. The rule
mismeasures how bad the smallest counts are, not where failure ends.

\subsection{The Count Narrows the Spread; the Sample Size Does Not}

\paragraph{Count sweep: the count narrows it, and it is still four times too wide.}
Every series narrows by 55 to 69\% from $k = 20$ to $k = 250$. The
narrowest cell still runs 0.194 wide against a reference of 0.0497
(Table~\ref{tab:fullpool}), nearly four times the quantity being estimated. What
low counts cost is precision and not location: the middle half of the estimates
still contains $\hat\beta_{\text{full}}$ in 39 of the 42 cells.

\paragraph{Size sweep: no sample size narrows it.}
Over a matched eightfold window, $n$ from 500 to 4000 against $k$ from 20 to
150, the sample size moves width between $-19\%$ and $+10\%$ with no consistent
sign, while the count over that shorter window still cuts it by 42 to 66\% in
all six series.

\paragraph{The rate explains the real arm, not the synthetic one.}
An interaction's variance scales as $1/k + 1/(n-k)$. Rescaled by that rate the
real width tracks at 0.97, and under the size sweep the rate predicts almost no
change, which is what the data shows. The synthetic arm sits above it at 1.14,
wider than the real arm in all nine comorbidity-by-count comparisons
(Figure~\ref{fig:ratio}; sign test $p = 0.004$).

\paragraph{A sign the sample size does not repair.}
COPD is the one comorbidity whose interaction is positive, $+0.0668$, and the
count sweep recovers that sign once $k$ passes 20. Holding the count at 20 does
not: the synthetic median stays negative at all five sample sizes, $-0.077$ to
$-0.159$. Sixteen times the data leaves the sign where twenty positives put
it.

\subsection{Synthesis Attenuates the Interaction, and No Count Repairs It}
\label{sec:sim}

Where MIMIC-IV has no truth to compare against, a simulated population does. It
is generated from a logistic model with a known
\texttt{chf}\,$\times$\,lactate interaction, then put through the same pipeline:
a subsample drawn at each count, synthesized, and both arms fitted as before.
The three true values sit well above the floor of Section~\ref{sec:scope}.

\paragraph{The larger the interaction, the more is lost.}
Synthesis keeps 99\% of a true value of 0.5, 59\% of 1.5 and 41\% of 3
(Table~\ref{tab:sim}), and no count on the grid closes the gap
(Figure~\ref{fig:simmedian}). The row at 0.5 is not the exception it looks like:
an estimator that pulls toward zero is accurate against any target near zero,
and the error columns show what the medians hide, 34\% larger on the synthetic
side where the medians agree to within 1\%. Nothing in the table reaches down to
the 0.050 to 0.071 MIMIC-IV offers, an order of magnitude below the smallest
value simulated.

    \centering

    \caption{The real arm converges on the truth; the synthetic arm lags at

\paragraph{The synthetic arm diverges less, not more.}
At a true value of 3 the real arm diverges in 21 to 29\% of replicates while the
synthetic arm diverges in 2.5 to 10.5\%, the reverse of the MIMIC-IV pattern. A
strong interaction puts the real subsample close to separation; the tree smooths
that away, so the synthetic fit converges more readily and converges on the
wrong value. The attenuation is bought, not incurred.

\subsection{The Split Diagnostic Is Unreadable Without Its Null}

What looks like recovery in the count sweep is class balance, not signal. The
diagnostic rises from 0 at $k = 5$ to 0.26 to 0.40 at $k = 250$, but the
permutation null rises with it, to as high as 0.32
(Figure~\ref{fig:tree}, left): the column grows from 1\% positive to 50\%, and a
balanced binary variable is easy to split on whether or not it carries signal
\cite{strobl2007}. Pooled across the grid the signal is still there, an odds
ratio of 2.0 ($p = 6\times10^{-6}$) from a conditional logistic model stratified
on (comorbidity, replicate) pairs, narrowing by 0.81 per doubling of the count
($p = 0.015$) as the null closes in.

Holding the count at 20 runs balance the other way, down to 0.5\% positive at
$n = 4000$. The null falls with it to near zero while the observed rate stays
put (Figure~\ref{fig:tree}, right), and with the null out of the way the signal
is legible without pooling: 104 discordant replicates to 32. The diagnostic is
readable where its null is near zero, which here means low prevalence.

The matched GLM reads the same way. It detects the comorbidity in 6.7 to 10.1\%
of replicates against the 6 to 7\% theory allows a 500-row subsample, and does
no better on real labels than on shuffled ones. Both methods are blind at these
sizes; CART is the one that fails visibly, by declining to spend a split.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The design, not the generator.}
The sweeps returned the floor of Section~\ref{sec:scope}: theory and the GLM arm
both land within a few points of the 5\% a powerless test would produce, which
puts the failure in the design and not in the pipeline. Neither sweep implicates
CART on its own.

\paragraph{A threshold, but not a cutoff.}
The count at which divergence stops sits in $(40, 80]$ for each series, but at
different points inside. That is the expected shape, not a ragged result, since
van Smeden et al.\ find no rationale for a fixed events-per-variable criterion
\cite{vansmeden2016}. A generator inherits a dependence on the count, not a
universal cutoff.

\paragraph{Three readings that disagree.}
Failure, spread and location do not move together. The synthetic arm spreads
wider than its own count predicts on MIMIC-IV and sits below the truth in the
simulation, yet at a large true effect it fails less often than the real one.
Read on its own that last reading looks like stability, and stability is what
the smoothing buys.

\paragraph{A privacy-fidelity tension.}
CART's reluctance to isolate a subgroup with few supporting cases is not simply a
limitation to work around. A tree willing to carve out a node of five patients
would also be likelier to overfit to and risk re-identifying them, and that same
reluctance leaves the stratum missing at $k = 5$. The tension belongs to the
method, not the implementation, though only its fidelity side is measured
here.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The diagnostic tree is a proxy for the generator: three predictors rather than
the full feature set \texttt{synthpop} synthesizes jointly, matched on control
parameters but not on visit order. Both are permissive, which makes
non-selection the more reliable outcome. Monte Carlo error at $M = 100$ reaches
13\% on interquartile widths, so a cell that misses significance is not evidence
that nothing is there. The single-copy GLM carries no correction for the
variance that synthesis contributes \cite{raab2016}, so the two arms are
compared as procedures in use, not as matched estimators. One continuous variable and one
outcome were examined, two of the five screened comorbidities were left
unanalysed, and all results come from a single MIMIC-IV snapshot.

\section{Conclusion}

The positive count governs every quantity the two sweeps measure and sample size
governs none: divergence falls to zero between counts of forty and eighty,
interquartile width falls by 42 to 66\% over a matched increase in the count, and
neither CART's use of the comorbidity nor a logistic fit to the same subsamples
moves with $n$. These are four readings on the same replicates rather than four
independent confirmations, but two of them never touch the synthesizer. In that
narrow sense the finding from Bayesian logistic regression extends to CART-based
synthesis.

The remedy does not carry over. The generator adds an attenuation no count
removes, partial even where the target is large enough to reach, and at clinical
effect sizes neither arm recovers the MIMIC-IV interactions at all: the narrowest
cell still spans nearly four times the quantity being estimated.

For synthetic data evaluation, two things follow: establish that the target
effect is estimable in the source data before attributing a discrepancy to the
generator, and calibrate any variable-selection diagnostic against a permutation
null. Neither is specific to \texttt{synthpop}: any generator that declines to
model a subgroup it has too few cases to support should show the same threshold,
and the two sweeps needed to find it require nothing beyond the source data and
the generator itself.
