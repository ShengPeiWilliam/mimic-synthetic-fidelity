\begin{abstract}
A logistic fit's precision is set by the number of positive cases rather than by
the sample size. This report asks whether the same threshold governs a
generator. CART-based synthesis (\texttt{synthpop}) is run on MIMIC-IV
($n = 70{,}954$ ICU stays) under two sweeps that hold the generator fixed and
vary only the input: one raises the rare stratum's positive count $k$ at fixed
sample size, the other raises the sample size $n$ at fixed $k$. Together they
separate count from prevalence.

The threshold does carry over, but the generator adds none of its own. Raising
$k$ drives divergence to zero between counts of forty and eighty, and over a
matched eightfold increase it cuts interquartile width by 42 to 66\% in all six
series, while the same increase in $n$ moves neither. Neither the real arm's
divergence rate nor a logistic fit to the same subsamples involves the
synthesizer at all, so the threshold belongs to the stratum and
\texttt{synthpop} inherits it rather than imposing a second one. What synthesis
contributes instead is a bias no count removes: in a companion simulation with a
known data-generating process, the synthetic median recovers 99\% of a true
interaction of 0.5 but only 41\% of one of 3. The finding transfers as a
mechanism and not as a remedy.

A 500-row subsample carries 6 to 7\% power against the interactions MIMIC-IV
offers, and the smallest one it could detect is seven to twelve times the one
actually present. Two recommendations follow: establish that a target effect is estimable in the
source data before attributing any discrepancy to the generator, and calibrate
variable-selection diagnostics against a permutation null. That null moves from
0.00 to 0.32 with class balance alone.
\end{abstract}

% -------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}

That a logistic fit's precision is set by the event count is established
\cite{peduzzi1996, vansmeden2016}, and separation at thin counts has been
characterised since \cite{albert1984}. In earlier coursework I found the same
under Bayesian logistic regression on the \texttt{birthwt} dataset: posterior
stability depends on a predictor's positive-case count rather than on overall
sample size. What is not established is whether the same threshold governs a
\emph{generator}. Whether a CART-based synthesizer preserves a rare-stratum
interaction is a question about which variables a tree spends a split on, not
about the curvature of a likelihood, and the two need not share a threshold.

Synthetic data is proposed as a way to share clinical records without exposing
patients, and is usually judged by general utility, how closely the synthetic
joint distribution resembles the real one \cite{snoke2018}. A generator can
score well on that and still lose the one coefficient a study was designed
around, so what follows holds the generator fixed and measures a single
interaction rather than overall similarity. The question matters because of a
specific misreading. A researcher handed a synthetic release of MIMIC-IV,
finding the CHF by creatinine interaction absent from it, would reasonably
conclude that the synthesizer had destroyed a clinical interaction. In the 500
rows in front of them that effect was never detectable to begin with, and the
same discrepancy would have appeared with no synthesizer involved at all.
Code is available at
\href{https://github.com/ShengPeiWilliam/mimic-synthetic-fidelity}{GitHub Repository}.

\section{Data}

\subsection{Cohort}

The cohort is MIMIC-IV v2.2 \cite{johnson2023}, restricted to hospital
admissions with an associated ICU stay, one row per stay. Dropping the 3\% of
stays with no first-day creatinine leaves a pool of 70{,}954 from which all
subsampling draws. In-hospital mortality in the pool is 10.9\%, so a 500-row
draw carries roughly 54 deaths against the five parameters each fit estimates.

Two features bound what the pool can serve as a reference for, and both land on
the full-pool estimate rather than on the sweeps. The dropped stays are not
missing at random, being far shorter and more than twice as often fatal, so the
reference is the interaction among stays that survived long enough to be
measured. And rows repeat patients, so full-pool standard errors are clustered
(Table~\ref{tab:fullpool}). The sweep comparison is untouched by both, drawing
its two arms from the same pool.

\subsection{Variables}

Three predictors enter every fit: age, first-day maximum creatinine, and one
comorbidity flag. Comorbidities are simplified ICD-9/ICD-10 prefix flags rather
than a validated index such as Elixhauser or Charlson, since the sweeps need a
binary flag with a known positive count. The three least prevalent of the five
screened were carried forward, COPD, CHF and Diabetes, running 11 to 30\%
positive in the pool; even the rarest is too common to supply a thin stratum on
its own, so the rare stratum is manufactured by subsampling rather than found in
the data. Creatinine is capped at the 99.5th percentile and left untransformed,
so every creatinine coefficient below is per mg/dL. The notebook carries the
screening table and the variable distributions.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Experimental Design}

Both sweeps draw stratified subsamples, synthesize each with
\texttt{synthpop::syn()} \cite{nowok2016}, and fit
\[
\texttt{hospital\_expire\_flag} \sim \texttt{age} + \texttt{creatinine} + \texttt{comorbidity} + \texttt{comorbidity}{:}\texttt{creatinine}
\]
to both the real subsample and the synthetic table, giving paired
estimates $\hat\beta_{\text{real}}$ and $\hat\beta_{\text{syn}}$ of the
interaction, at $M = 100$ Monte Carlo replicates per grid point. The size sweep
holds the count at 20, the last value at which it still binds, so that if
sample size can substitute for count this is where it would show
(Table~\ref{tab:design}).

\centering

Sweep & Held fixed & Varied & What moves \\
\midrule
Count sweep    & $n = 500$ & $k \in \{5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 150, 250\}$
                  & count and prevalence together \\
Size sweep     & $k = 20$  & $n \in \{250, 500, 1000, 2000, 4000\}$
                  & prevalence only, 8\% to 0.5\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The two sweeps. Each holds the generator fixed and varies one input.}
\label{tab:design}
\end{table}

Throughout, a \emph{series} is one comorbidity in one arm, six in all, and a
\emph{cell} is one series at one grid point.

\subsection{Analyses}

Two analyses run on every grid point. The first compares
$\hat\beta_{\text{real}}$ against $\hat\beta_{\text{syn}}$ on the median and
interquartile width of the converged estimates, with the divergence and
inestimable rates kept separate; Section~\ref{sec:failure} defines the two ways
a fit fails. Throughout, the synthetic arm is fitted the way a data recipient
would fit it: one synthetic copy, an ordinary GLM.

The second is a tree diagnosis. It refits the subsamples both sweeps draw
with \texttt{rpart}, under the control parameters
\texttt{synthpop::syn.cart} uses, and records whether the comorbidity earns a
split. That is read against a likelihood-ratio test on the same rows, dropping
the comorbidity and its interaction together, so both are asked the same
question. Every configuration is re-run with the
comorbidity column shuffled, which leaves the positive count intact and destroys
its association with the outcome, supplying the null.

\subsection{Two Failure Modes}
\label{sec:failure}

Fits at the smallest counts fail often enough that the rates are reported as a
result rather than used as a filtering step. A fit is \textbf{inestimable} when
the table holds no positives and the interaction returns \texttt{NA};
\textbf{divergent} when the term is estimated but runs past $|2|$, forty times
the full-pool estimate, marking a likelihood maximized at the boundary
\cite{heinze2002}. Only divergence appears in the real arm, which holds exactly
$k$ positives by construction; the synthetic table generates its own column and
can produce none.

Rates are shares of all $M$ replicates, so converged, inestimable and divergent
sum to one; every quantile below uses converged fits only.

\subsection{The Full-Pool Reference}

MIMIC-IV carries no known ground truth, so the interaction fit on the whole
pool stands in as the reference, $\hat\beta_{\text{full}}$, and every subsample
estimate is read against it (Table~\ref{tab:fullpool}). The three interactions
differ in sign, CHF and Diabetes dampening creatinine's main effect while COPD
steepens it, and all three are roughly three to four times smaller than that
main effect.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{What This Design Can and Cannot Resolve}
\label{sec:scope}

The reference also bounds what the subsamples can show. At $n = 500$ the
smallest interaction detectable at 80\% power is 0.47 to 0.78, against the 0.050
to 0.071 the pool actually holds, seven to twelve times too coarse; reaching
those effects would need roughly 23{,}000 to 67{,}000 rows.

No subsample of the size studied here can therefore say whether synthesis
recovers a true interaction. That is a trade rather than a concession. What the
two sweeps measure instead is precision and failure behaviour, how wide the
estimates run and how often a fit cannot be produced at all, and both are
readable at any effect size, including one too small to detect. Every claim
about bias rests on the simulation of Section~\ref{sec:sim}, where the truth is
known and large enough to reach.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Count, Not Sample Size, Removes Divergence}

\paragraph{Count sweep.}
Divergence runs at 18 to 40\% at $k = 5$ and every series is at zero by $k = 80$
(Figure~\ref{fig:nonconv}, left),
though not at one shared count: two are already there at $k = 40$, and a zero
over 100 replicates bounds a rate at 3.6\% rather than establishing it as nil,
so the threshold sits in $(40, 80]$ for each series. Neither prevalence nor the
sign of the interaction moves it.

\paragraph{Size sweep.}
No sample size does the same. Over a matched eightfold increase in $n$ no series
comes near zero (Figure~\ref{fig:nonconv}, right). What separates cleanly here is the arm rather than the size:
the synthetic rate is the higher of the two in fourteen of the fifteen
comorbidity-by-size comparisons, and no sample size closes the gap.

    sample size (right); note the differing vertical scales. The two smallest

\paragraph{Detection floor.}
The $|2|$ cutoff understates divergence at the smallest counts. A penalized fit
\cite{firth1993} finds 64 to 76\% of real fits separated at $k = 5$ where $|2|$
flags 18 to 34\%, because \texttt{glm} stops after twenty-five iterations rather
than running to the boundary. The two agree from $k = 20$ up, and the penalized
fit removes the divergence without moving the count at which it stops. What the
cutoff mismeasures is how bad the smallest counts are, not where failure ends.

\subsection{The Count Narrows the Spread; the Sample Size Does Not}

\paragraph{Count sweep.}
Spread narrows steadily with the count, every series losing 55 to 69\% of its
width from $k = 20$ to $k = 250$. It is still not enough: the narrowest cell has
an interquartile width of 0.194 against its reference of 0.0497
(Table~\ref{tab:fullpool}), nearly four times the quantity being estimated. What
low counts cost is precision and not location, the middle half of the estimates
still containing $\hat\beta_{\text{full}}$ in 39 of the 42 cells
(Figure~\ref{fig:ribbon}, left).

\paragraph{Size sweep.}
Over the matched eightfold window, $k$ from 20 to 150 against $n$ from 500 to
4000, the count cuts width by 42 to 66\% in all six series while the sample size
changes it by between $-19\%$ and $+10\%$ with no consistent sign
(Figure~\ref{fig:ribbon}, right); the count's figure falls below the 55 to 69\%
above only because this window stops at $k = 150$. Across the full sixteen-fold
range of $n$ the widths never leave the band the count sweep recorded at
$k = 20$.

\paragraph{What the count rate explains.}
An interaction's variance scales as $1/k + 1/(n-k)$, and that rate accounts for
both panels. Rescaling the $k = 250$ width by it tracks the real arm at 0.97
across the count sweep; with $k$ held at 20 the $1/k$ term cannot move, so it
predicts widths within 4\% across the whole size range, against observed scatter
several times that in both directions. What it does not account for is the
synthetic arm, which sits below the real one and spreads wider than its own
count predicts: the same rescaling puts it at 1.14, higher than the real arm at
every one of the nine matched cells (Figure~\ref{fig:ratio}; sign test
$p = 0.004$).

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering

    \caption{The real arm tracks the width its count predicts; the synthetic
    arm sits above the real one in all nine matched cells.}
    \label{fig:ratio}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{A sign that sample size does not repair.}
Width is not the only thing sample size fails to fix. COPD is the one
comorbidity whose interaction is positive, $+0.0668$, and the count sweep
recovers that sign once $k$ passes 20. Holding the count at 20 does
not: the synthetic median is negative at all five sample sizes, from
$-0.077$ to $-0.159$, matching the $-0.177$ recorded by the count sweep at the
same count. Sixteen times the data leaves the sign where twenty positives
put it.

\subsection{What No Count Fixes: Attenuation Under a Known Truth}
\label{sec:sim}

As Section~\ref{sec:scope} set out, MIMIC-IV offers no truth to compare
against and no power to reach one. A companion simulation supplies both: a population is
generated from a logistic model with a known \texttt{chf}\,$\times$\,lactate
interaction, and the same count sweep is run at three true values, 200
replicates per cell.

Synthesis loses more of the interaction the larger it is. At a true value of
3 the real arm recovers 2.75 and the synthetic arm 1.22 (Table~\ref{tab:sim}),
and no count on the grid closes that gap (Figure~\ref{fig:simmedian}). The row at 0.5 is not the exception
it looks like: an estimator that pulls toward zero is accurate against any
target near zero, and the error columns show the difference the medians hide,
34\% larger on the synthetic side where the two medians agree to within 1\%.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.45\textwidth]{../figures/sim_median.png}

At a true value of 3 the real arm diverges in 21 to 29\% of replicates while the
synthetic arm diverges in 2.5 to 10.5\%, the reverse of the MIMIC-IV pattern. A
strong interaction puts the real subsample close to separation; the tree smooths
that away, so the synthetic fit converges more readily and converges on the
wrong value. The attenuation is bought, not incurred.

\subsection{The Split Diagnostic Is Unreadable Without Its Null}

What looks like recovery in the count sweep is class balance, not signal. The
diagnostic rises from 0 at $k = 5$ to 0.26 to 0.40 at $k = 250$, but the
permutation null rises with it, to as high as 0.32
(Figure~\ref{fig:treecount}), since what grows alongside the count is the
balance of the column itself, from 1\% positive to 50\%, and a balanced binary
variable is easy to split on whether or not it carries signal
\cite{strobl2007}. Pooled across the grid the signal is still there. A
conditional logistic model, \texttt{used \textasciitilde{} observed +
observed:log2k\_c + strata(pair)}, stratified on (comorbidity, replicate) pairs
with $\log_2 k$ centred at $k = 40$, puts the real column ahead of the shuffled
one by an odds ratio of 2.0 ($p = 6\times10^{-6}$), narrowing by 0.81 per
doubling of the count ($p = 0.015$) as the null closes in.

Holding the count at 20 runs class balance the other way, down to 0.5\% positive
at $n = 4000$. The null falls with it to near zero while the observed rate stays
where the count sweep left it (Figure~\ref{fig:treesize}), and with the null out
of the way the signal is legible without pooling: the real column wins 104
discordant replicates to 32. The diagnostic is readable where its null is near
zero, which on this data means low prevalence.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The design, not the generator.}
The floor set out in Section~\ref{sec:scope} is what the sweeps returned. Theory
gives a 500-row subsample 6 to 7\% power against these interactions and the GLM
arm returned 6.7 to 10.1\%, both within a few points of the 5\% a powerless test
would produce; the two landing together rules out a defect in the pipeline and
locates the failure in the design. Nor
does either sweep implicate CART on its own, since the GLM does no better on
real labels than on shuffled ones; CART is only the method that fails visibly,
by declining to spend a split.

\paragraph{A threshold, but not a cutoff.}
The count at which divergence stops is not one number: it sits in $(40, 80]$ for
each series but lands at different points inside that window. That is the
expected shape rather than a ragged result, since van Smeden et al.\ find no
rationale for a fixed events-per-variable criterion \cite{vansmeden2016}. What
transfers to a generator is a dependence on the count, not a universal cutoff.

\paragraph{A privacy-fidelity tension.}
CART's reluctance to isolate a subgroup with few supporting cases is not simply
a limitation to work around. A tree willing to carve out a node of five patients
would also be likelier to overfit to, and risk re-identifying, them, and that
same reluctance leaves the stratum missing from the synthetic table at $k = 5$.
The tension is a property of the method, not an implementation defect, though
only its fidelity side is measured here.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The diagnostic tree is a proxy for the generator: three predictors rather than
the full feature set \texttt{synthpop} synthesizes jointly, matched on control
parameters but not on its sequential visit order. Both are permissive, which
makes non-selection the more reliable outcome. Monte Carlo error at $M = 100$
reaches 13\% on interquartile widths, so a cell that misses significance is not
evidence that nothing is there. The single-copy GLM on the synthetic side
carries no correction for the variance synthesis contributes; proper inference
uses several copies and a combining rule \cite{raab2016}, so the two arms are
compared as procedures in use rather than as matched estimators. One continuous
variable and one outcome were examined, two of the five screened comorbidities
were left unanalysed, the pool excludes the short, high-mortality stays that
carry no first-day creatinine, and all results come from a single MIMIC-IV
snapshot.

\section{Conclusion}

The positive count governs every quantity the two sweeps measure and sample
size governs none: divergence falls to zero between counts of forty and eighty, interquartile
width falls by 42 to 66\% over a matched increase in the count, and neither CART's
use of the comorbidity nor a logistic fit to the same subsamples moves with $n$.
These are four readings on the same replicates rather than four independent
confirmations, but two of them come from data the synthesizer never touched. In
that narrow sense the finding from Bayesian logistic regression does extend to
CART-based synthesis.

What does not extend is a remedy. The generator adds an attenuation no count
removes, and the simulation shows it partial even where the target is large
enough to reach. At clinical effect sizes neither arm recovers the
MIMIC-IV interactions at all, the narrowest cell still spanning nearly four
times the quantity being estimated.

For synthetic data evaluation, two things follow: establish that the target
effect is estimable in the source data before attributing a discrepancy to the
generator, and calibrate any variable-selection diagnostic against a permutation
null. Neither is specific to \texttt{synthpop}: any generator that declines to
model a subgroup it has too few cases to support should show the same threshold,
and the two sweeps needed to find it require nothing beyond the source data and
the generator itself.
