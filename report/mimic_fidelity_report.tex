\begin{abstract}
A logistic fit's precision is set by the number of positive cases rather
than by the sample size. This report asks whether the same threshold
governs a generator, using CART-based synthesis (\texttt{synthpop}) on
MIMIC-IV ($n = 70{,}954$ ICU stays) and two sweeps that hold the generator
fixed and vary only the input: one raises the rare stratum's positive
count $k$ at fixed sample size, the other raises the sample size $n$ at
fixed $k$, which together separate count from prevalence.
It does, but the generator adds no threshold of its own. Over increases of
comparable size, raising $k$ drives divergence to zero between counts of
forty and eighty and cuts interquartile width by 42--66\% in all six
series, while raising $n$ moves neither. Two of the four quantities never
involve the synthesizer at all, so the threshold belongs to the stratum and
\texttt{synthpop} inherits it rather than imposing a second one. What
synthesis contributes instead is a bias no count removes: in a companion
simulation with a known data-generating process, the synthetic median
recovers 99\% of a true interaction of 0.5 but only 41\% of one of 3, at a
count where neither arm fails.
The finding transfers as a mechanism and not as a remedy. The interactions
available in MIMIC-IV are small enough that a 500-row subsample carries
6--7\% power to detect them, which is what the sweeps returned: the smallest
interaction such a subsample could detect is seven to twelve times the one
MIMIC-IV holds, and eighty percent power would need 23{,}384 to 67{,}238
rows. No count reachable inside a subsample of this size makes the estimate
usable.
Two recommendations follow: establish that a target effect is estimable
in the source data before attributing any discrepancy to the generator,
and calibrate variable-selection diagnostics against a permutation null,
whose own rate here moves from 0.00 to 0.32 with class balance alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Synthetic data is often proposed as a way to share clinical records
without exposing patients, but whether it preserves the relationships
researchers care about is a separate question from whether it looks
realistic overall.
A prior analysis of the \texttt{birthwt} dataset found that under Bayesian
logistic regression, posterior stability depends on the number of positive
cases for a predictor rather than on overall sample size. That a logistic
fit's precision is set by the event count is itself established
\cite{peduzzi1996, vansmeden2016}, and the conditions under which small
event counts produce separation have been characterised since
\cite{albert1984}. What is not established is whether the same threshold
governs a \emph{generator}. Whether a CART-based synthesizer preserves a
rare-stratum interaction is a question about which variables a tree spends
a split on, not about the curvature of a likelihood, and the two need not
share a threshold.

This report asks whether the same mechanism holds for CART-based
sequential synthesis. The generator is held fixed throughout; the
question is not which tree-based method performs best but how one of
them behaves when the stratum it must reproduce is small. Because
positive count and prevalence move together whenever sample size is
fixed, a single sweep cannot attribute a change to either, so two
complementary sweeps are run.
Code is available at
\href{https://github.com/ShengPeiWilliam/mimic-synthetic-fidelity}{GitHub Repository}.

\section{Data}

\subsection{Cohort}

MIMIC-IV v2.2 \cite{johnson2023}, restricted to hospital admissions with
an associated ICU stay. One row per ICU stay (73{,}181 stays) was built from
\texttt{icustays}, \texttt{patients}, \texttt{admissions}, and
\texttt{diagnoses\_icd}, the last supplying the comorbidity flags.
Dropping the 3\% of stays with no first-day creatinine leaves 70{,}954
rows; all subsampling draws from this pool. Those 2{,}227 stays are not
missing at random. They die in hospital at 28.1\% against 10.9\% for the
stays kept, their median length of stay is 0.73 days against 1.96, and they
carry every comorbidity less often: CHF 15.4\% against 27.7\%, Diabetes
21.7\% against 30.5\%, COPD 7.9\% against 11.3\%. The pattern is the one a
first-day lab implies. A stay that ends within hours, by death or by
discharge, has neither the time for the draw nor the encounter for the
coding, so the lower comorbidity rates record thinner documentation rather
than healthier patients. The pool is conditioned on surviving long enough to
be measured, and $\hat\beta_{\text{full}}$ is accordingly the interaction
among stays that clear that bar rather than among ICU stays at large. Both
arms of every sweep are drawn from the same pool, so the comparison this
report rests on is unaffected; what the selection limits is the clinical
reading of the reference. Rows are not independent:
the outcome and the comorbidity flags are recorded per admission, and the
70{,}954 stays span 64{,}308 admissions and 49{,}563 patients, so 30\% of
rows repeat a patient. Standard errors on the full-pool fit are clustered
on patient. Age is MIMIC-IV's
\texttt{anchor\_age}, which is the age at the anchor year rather than at
admission and is censored above 89.

\subsection{Comorbidity Screening}

Five comorbidities were defined by simplified ICD-9/ICD-10 prefix
groupings rather than a validated index such as Elixhauser or Charlson,
since what the sweeps need is a binary flag with a known positive count
rather than a calibrated severity score. They are counted by admission rather than by ICU stay, the unit the pool is indexed
on: COPD 7{,}244, CHF 17{,}412, Diabetes 19{,}806, Renal Failure 25{,}369
and Hypertension 34{,}979. The three least common were carried forward.
All five are common in the pool; the rare stratum is created by the
subsampling below, not found in the data.

\subsection{Continuous Variable Selection}

Creatinine was chosen from five first-day lab candidates for its 97\%
coverage and its link to CHF through cardiorenal syndrome. The maximum in the first 24 hours was taken, capped at the 99.5th
percentile ($11$~mg/dL) and left untransformed, so every creatinine
coefficient below is per mg/dL.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

Both sweeps draw stratified subsamples, synthesize each with
\texttt{synthpop::syn()} \cite{nowok2016}, and fit
\[
\texttt{hospital\_expire\_flag} \sim \texttt{age} + \texttt{creatinine} + \texttt{comorbidity} + \texttt{comorbidity}{:}\texttt{creatinine}
\]
to both the real subsample and the synthetic table, giving paired
estimates $\hat\beta_{\text{real}}$ and $\hat\beta_{\text{syn}}$ of the
interaction, at $M = 100$ Monte Carlo replicates per grid point.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Experiment & Held fixed & Varied & What moves \\
\midrule
1. Count sweep    & $n = 500$ & $k \in \{5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 150, 250\}$
                  & count and prevalence together \\
2. Size sweep     & $k = 20$  & $n \in \{250, 500, 1000, 2000, 4000\}$
                  & prevalence only, 8\% to 0.5\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Analyses}

We conduct three analyses. (1) A full-pool fit of the interaction supplies
the reference every subsample estimate is read against. (2) The two sweeps
compare $\hat\beta_{\text{real}}$ against $\hat\beta_{\text{syn}}$ at each
grid point on the median and interquartile width of the converged
estimates, with the divergence and inestimable rates kept separate;
Section~\ref{sec:failure} defines the two ways a fit fails. (3) A tree
diagnosis refits the subsamples both sweeps draw with \texttt{rpart} under
\texttt{synthpop::syn.cart}'s control parameters and records whether the
comorbidity earns a split, against a likelihood-ratio test on the same
rows that drops the comorbidity and its interaction together, so both are
asked the same question. Every configuration is re-run with the
comorbidity column shuffled, which leaves the positive count intact and
destroys its association with the outcome, supplying the null. The two
columns are compared across the whole grid by a conditional logistic model,
\texttt{used \textasciitilde{} observed + observed:log2k\_c + strata(pair)},
whose strata are the (comorbidity, replicate) pairs holding one observed and
one shuffled outcome each, with $\log_2 k$ centred at $k = 40$ so that the
main effect is read there; the fit runs over 4{,}200 rows and 585 splits.

Throughout, the synthetic arm is fitted the way an analyst who has been handed
one synthetic file would fit it: a single synthetic copy, an ordinary GLM, and
no correction for the variance synthesis itself contributes. Proper inference
from synthetic data uses several copies and a combining rule
\cite{raab2016}, which would widen the synthetic intervals reported here. The
naive fit is the one measured because it is the one a data recipient
performs, but the comparison against the real arm is therefore between
procedures as used, not between estimators of matched validity.

\section{Estimation Failure}
\label{sec:failure}

\subsection{Two Failure Modes}

Fits at the smallest counts failed often enough to matter, and the
failures turned out to be of two distinct kinds. We report their rates as
a result rather than a filtering step: whether an estimate can be produced
at all separates the two levers more sharply than the spread of those that
succeed. Every quantile that follows is computed on converged fits only.

A fit is \textbf{inestimable} when the table holds no positives and the
interaction term comes back as \texttt{NA}; \textbf{divergent} when the
term is estimated but runs past $|2|$, marking a likelihood maximized at
the boundary \cite{heinze2002} rather than a real effect. Only divergence
appears in the real arm, which is built to hold exactly $k$ positives; the
synthetic table generates its own column and can produce none.

The cutoff follows the scale of the target, around $0.05$ in the full
pool, and recomputing at 5 and 10 leaves every comparison pointing the
same way. It under-detects at the smallest counts: at $k = 5$ between 64\%
and 76\% of real fits are separated, which a penalized fit
\cite{firth1993} confirms, while $|2|$ flags 18--34\% of them, since
\texttt{glm} stops after twenty-five iterations rather than running to the
boundary. From $k = 20$ up the two agree.
Both modes are reported as shares of all $M$ replicates, so converged,
inestimable and divergent sum to one.

\paragraph{Count sweep.}
Every series is at zero by $k = 80$, but not at the same count: the CHF and
COPD real arms are already there at $k = 40$, where the other four sit at
2--4\%, two to four replicates out of a hundred. Each zero is 0 of 100,
which bounds a rate at 3.6\% rather than establishing it as nil, so what
the data supports is a threshold somewhere in $(40, 80]$ for each series
rather than one shared value. Wherever it sits, it moves with neither
prevalence, which varies nearly threefold across the three comorbidities,
nor the sign of the interaction. Below it, divergence runs
at 18--40\% at $k = 5$ and is higher on the synthetic side in ten of the
twelve failing cells. Both exceptions are COPD, at $k = 5$ and $k = 10$,
where the real arm is the one that fails more often;
Section~\ref{sec:sim} reproduces that reversal under a known truth and
gives it a mechanism. The inestimable rate is narrower still: synthetic only,
$k = 5$ only, at 4--6\%.

\paragraph{Size sweep.}
No sample size does the same. Over the matched 8-fold increase in $n$
every series changes by between $-29\%$ and $+25\%$, and none comes near
zero (Figure~\ref{fig:nonconv}, right). COPD is the plainest case: its
real arm fails at 12--14\% at every one of the five sample sizes.
What does separate cleanly under this lever is the arm rather than the
size: the synthetic rate is the higher of the two in fourteen of the
fifteen cells and tied in the fifteenth, by 3 to 19 points, and no sample
size closes the gap.
The inestimable rate is zero in all fifteen cells, including at $n = 2000$,
where prevalence matches the $k = 5$ cells that lost the stratum 4--6\% of
the time. Failure follows the count, and only the count.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Experiment 1: Count Sweep}

Spread narrows steadily with the count: from $k = 20$ to $k = 250$ every
one of the six series loses 55\% to 69\% of its width, and none has
flattened by the end of the range. It is still not enough. Even the
narrowest cell, the CHF real arm at $k = 250$, has an interquartile width
of 0.194 against its reference of 0.0497 (Table~\ref{tab:power}), nearly
four times the quantity being estimated. The low end of the span rests on
fewer replicates, 6\% to 44\% being discarded below $k = 40$, but all of
them for diverging, so the decline is if anything understated.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{../figures/spread_ribbon.png}
    \caption{Comorbidity $\times$ creatinine, real vs.\ synthetic, plotted as
    distance from $\hat\beta_{\text{full}}$ so that zero is the full-pool
    value in all three panels. Band = middle 50\% of converged fits.}
    \label{fig:spread}
\end{figure}

What low counts cost is precision, not location: in 39 of the 42 cells the
middle half of the estimates still contains $\hat\beta_{\text{full}}$, the
three exceptions all COPD at $k \leq 10$. The two arms differ on both
counts, and in the same direction. The synthetic median sits below the
real one in 17 of the 21 counts, and the synthetic spread is the wider of
the two.

How much wider is worth stating against a rate rather than against the
real arm. The variance of an interaction draws on both groups, so it
scales as $1/k + 1/(n-k)$, and holding $n$ at 500 means raising $k$ also
thins the negatives. Rescaling the $k = 250$ width by that factor
reproduces the real arm closely: the ratio of observed to predicted has a
median of 0.97 and stays within 0.85 and 1.07 across the nine cells at
$k \in \{40, 80, 150\}$; the $k = 250$ anchor is fixed at 1 by construction,
and counts below 40 are excluded because divergence discards too many
replicates there for a width to mean anything. The synthetic arm sits
higher, median 1.14 against 0.97 and maximum 1.35, and the comparison that
carries the weight is the paired one rather than the count of cells above 1:
at every one of the nine matched cells, same comorbidity and same count, the
synthetic ratio exceeds the real one (Figure~\ref{fig:ratio}; sign test
$p = 0.004$). The count accounts for the real spread. The synthetic arm
carries a further excess it does not explain, at every count where both arms
can be read.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{../figures/width_vs_theory.png}
    \caption{Interquartile width against the width the $1/k + 1/(n-k)$ rate
    predicts from the $k = 250$ anchor, which is fixed at 1 by construction and
    omitted. The real arm tracks the rate; the synthetic arm sits above it in
    six of the nine cells, with ranges that overlap.}
    \label{fig:ratio}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Experiment 2: Size Sweep}

The narrowing Experiment 1 found does not reappear under the other lever.
Over roughly eightfold increases in each, $k$ from 20 to 150 and $n$ from
500 to 4000, the count cuts width by 42\% to 66\% in all six series while
the sample size changes it by between $-19\%$ and $+10\%$ with no
consistent sign (Figure~\ref{fig:size}). Across the full sixteen-fold
range of $n$ the widths never leave the band Experiment 1 recorded at
$k = 20$.

The count was held at 20, the last configuration in which it still binds:
failure there runs 6--17\% of replicates against 0--4\% at $k = 40$ and none
from $k = 80$ up. If sample size can substitute for count, this is where
it would show.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{../figures/size_ribbon.png}
    \caption{Comorbidity $\times$ creatinine with the positive count held at
    20, on the same scale as Figure~\ref{fig:spread}. In the COPD panel the
    synthetic band sits below zero at every $n$ while the real one
    straddles it.}
    \label{fig:size}
\end{figure}

Width is not the only thing sample size fails to fix. COPD is the one
comorbidity whose interaction is positive, $+0.0668$, and Experiment 1
recovers that sign once the count passes 20. Holding the count at 20 does
not: the synthetic median is negative at all five sample sizes, from
$-0.077$ to $-0.159$, matching the $-0.177$ Experiment 1 recorded at the
same count. Sixteen times the data leaves the sign where twenty positives
put it.

The size effect itself cannot be resolved from noise. The configuration
$k = 20$, $n = 500$ appears in both experiments and so is measured twice
under independent seeds; the readings differ by 13\%, 10\% and 0.3\%, the
same order as the variation across $n$.

\subsection{A Simulation with Known Truth}
\label{sec:sim}

MIMIC-IV cannot say whether synthesis attenuates the interaction, since
there is no truth to compare against and, at these effect sizes, no power
to find one. A companion simulation supplies both: a population is
generated from a logistic model with a known \texttt{chf}\,$\times$\,lactate
interaction, and the same count sweep is run at three true values, 200
replicates per cell.

The count threshold holds at the two smaller effect sizes, and the synthetic
arm clears it later than the real one at both: divergence reaches zero at
$k = 40$ against $k = 80$ at a true value of 0.5, and at $k = 150$ against
$k = 250$ at 1.5. At a true value of 3 neither arm reaches zero anywhere on
the grid, and the real rate is not even monotone, rising from 10.5\% at
$k = 5$ to 29\% at $k = 20$ before falling to 0.5\% at $k = 250$. The reason
is the cutoff itself: an estimate pinned near zero cannot exceed it, so at low
counts a strong interaction is missed rather than blown up. Convergence there
is not success. At $k = 20$ the 71\% of real replicates that converge have a
median of $-0.201$ against a truth of 3. What the simulation adds is
the size of what survives, and it is not a fixed fraction: the synthetic
median recovers 99\% of a true value of 0.5, 59\% of 1.5 and 41\% of 3
(Table~\ref{tab:sim}). The shortfall widens as the target grows and the
absolute gap widens faster, from 0.004 to 1.78.

The row at 0.5 is therefore not evidence that small interactions survive
synthesis. An estimator that saturates looks accurate against any target
small enough, and this one saturates: the three synthetic medians sit close
to $0.70\sqrt{\text{true}}$ across the range. The per-replicate errors say
the same thing without the fit: at a true value of 0.5 the two medians agree
to within 1\%, but the median absolute error is 0.219 on the synthetic side
against 0.164 on the real, 34\% larger. A median that lands on the truth is
not the same as estimates that do. Nothing in the table extends
to the 0.050 to 0.071 MIMIC-IV offers, an order of magnitude below the
smallest value simulated. Recomputing the divergence cutoff at 5, 10, 20
and 40 moves the shortfall by at most five points.

Attenuation is bought, not incurred. At a true value of 3 the real arm
diverges in 21--29\% of replicates between $k = 20$ and $k = 80$ while the
synthetic arm diverges in 2.5--10.5\%, the reverse of the pattern on
MIMIC-IV everywhere but the two COPD cells of Figure~\ref{fig:nonconv}. A strong interaction puts the real subsample close to
separation; the tree smooths that away, so the synthetic fit converges
more readily and converges on the wrong value. At $k = 80$ the real median
is 2.85 against a truth of 3, and the synthetic median is $-0.150$.

Below the threshold the count governs the sign and not only the spread. The
real median carries the wrong sign through $k = 5$ at a true value of 0.5,
through $k = 10$ at 1.5 and through $k = 20$ at 3; the synthetic median stays
wrong one to two counts longer in each case, through $k = 40$, $k = 40$ and
$k = 80$. The larger the interaction, the longer the median points the wrong
way, which is the same mechanism read from the other side: a strong effect
drives more of the low-count replicates to the boundary, and the ones left
converging are the ones that did not find it. It is the pattern the COPD
panel of Figure~\ref{fig:size} shows on MIMIC-IV, where twenty positives
hold the synthetic median at the wrong sign through a sixteenfold increase
in $n$.

    \caption{Median estimate against the positive count at three true values,
    dashed line the truth in each panel. Table~\ref{tab:sim} reports the

Taken with the MIMIC-IV sweeps the picture is consistent in one direction
and not the other. The count threshold appears wherever it can be looked
for, at simulated effect sizes and at clinical ones. Usable recovery
appears only at simulated ones.

\subsection{Tree Diagnosis: The Diagnostic Needs a Null}

\subsubsection*{Count sweep}

What looks like recovery is class balance, not signal. Read without a
control the diagnostic rises from 0 at $k = 5$ to 0.26--0.40 at
$k = 250$, but the permutation null rises with it, from 0.00 to as high as
0.32 (Table~\ref{tab:tree}). What grows alongside the count is the balance
of the column itself, from 1\% positive at $k = 5$ to 50\% at $k = 250$,
and a balanced binary variable is structurally easy to split on whether or
not it carries signal \cite{strobl2007}. Any diagnostic of this form
requires permutation calibration before it can be read. The rates in both
tables are proportions over 100 replicates and are less precise than they
look: 0.27 carries a 95\% interval of $[0.19, 0.37]$, and 0.00 bounds the
rate at 0.036 rather than establishing it as nil.

Read across the grid rather than cell by cell, the effect is clear. A
conditional logistic model with each replicate as its own stratum puts the
real column ahead of the shuffled one by an odds ratio of 2.0 at $k = 40$
($p = 6\times10^{-6}$), and that advantage narrows as the count rises, by a
factor of 0.81 per doubling ($p = 0.015$). Fitted across the sweep it runs
from 3.7 at $k = 5$ to 1.2 at $k = 250$, which is the null closing on the
observed rate rather than the observed rate pulling away. CHF and COPD show
both terms on their own; Diabetes shows neither.

The same data supports much less one cell at a time. Most replicates are
concordant and carry no information about which column is better, so each
paired test turns on a handful of discordant pairs: $p < 0.01$ in two of
the 21 cells, one surviving a Bonferroni correction, against 249 discordant
replicates to 164 when the grid is pooled.

\subsubsection*{Size sweep}

Holding the count at 20 runs class balance the other way, from 8\%
positive at $n = 250$ to 0.5\% at $n = 4000$, and the null falls with it,
ending between 0.00 and 0.03 (Table~\ref{tab:treesize}). The null
therefore tracks the balance of the column and nothing else, in a range
the count sweep never reached. The observed rate does not follow it down,
staying between 0.04 and 0.14 at every size, where the count sweep left it
at $k = 20$.

With the null near zero the signal becomes legible. Pooled over this grid
the real column wins 104 discordant replicates to 32, and 14 of the 15
cells point the same way, against 249 to 164 in the count sweep. Per cell
the tests stay underpowered, only CHF at $n = 1000$ surviving a Bonferroni
correction over 15 comparisons, but the direction is no longer in doubt:
the diagnostic is readable where its null is near zero, which on this data
means low prevalence.

Neither sweep implicates CART on its own. A GLM fitted to the same
subsamples detects the comorbidity in 2--16\% of replicates in every cell
of both sweeps, against the 6--7\% predicted by rescaling the full-pool
standard errors to $n = 500$, and it does no better on real labels than on
shuffled ones: over the size sweep
the observed rate averages 9.1\% and the permuted 9.5\%, with no trend in
$n$. Both methods are blind at these sizes; CART is the one that fails
visibly, by declining to spend a split.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Two failure modes, not one.}
Reporting the failure rates separately from the spread changed the reading
of this data more than any other decision. Mixing them inflates apparent
instability at low counts, since divergent fits push the quantiles
outward, and it hides the clearest result in the study, a
failure threshold that every series reaches between $k = 40$ and $k = 80$.

\paragraph{A privacy-fidelity tension.}
CART's reluctance to isolate a subgroup with few supporting cases is not
simply a limitation to work around. A tree willing to carve out a node of
five patients would also be more likely to overfit to, and risk
re-identifying, those individuals, and that same reluctance is what leaves
the stratum missing from the synthetic table in the inestimable cases at
$k = 5$. The tension is a property of the method rather than an
implementation defect, though only its fidelity side is measured here.

\paragraph{The design, not the generator.}
A 500-row subsample carries 6--7\% power against these interactions and the
GLM arm returned 6.7--10.1\%, both within a few points of the 5\% a test
with no power at all would produce. Theory and experiment landing in the
same place is what rules out a defect in the pipeline and locates the
failure in the design rather than in the generator. The size of the gap is
easier to read as a detectable effect than as a rate: at $n = 500$ the
smallest interaction detectable at 80\% power is 0.472 for CHF, 0.485 for
Diabetes and 0.775 for COPD, against the 0.0497, 0.0709 and 0.0668 actually
present, a factor of 9.5, 6.8 and 11.6. Reaching those interactions instead
of that floor would need between 23{,}384 and 67{,}238 rows. The floor also
explains a choice in Section~\ref{sec:sim} that would otherwise look
arbitrary: the smallest interaction put into the simulated
data-generating process, 0.5, is of the same order as the smallest this
design can detect at all, so the simulation starts where MIMIC-IV's
detectable range ends. The two data-generating processes differ, so the
floors are comparable in scale and not in value.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The diagnostic tree is a proxy for the generator: three predictors rather
than the full feature set \texttt{synthpop} synthesizes jointly, matched
on control parameters but not on its sequential visit order, and counting
the comorbidity as used wherever it appears rather than requiring it
beneath a creatinine split. All three are permissive, which makes non-selection the
more reliable of the two outcomes. Monte Carlo error at $M = 100$ reaches 13\% on
interquartile widths, and the paired tree tests turn on at most 40
discordant replicates per cell in the count sweep and 14 in the size
sweep, so a cell that
misses significance is not evidence that nothing is there. Divergence at the smallest counts is largely separation: a penalized fit
removes 60--70\% of it at $k = 5$ and most of the rest by $k = 20$, but it
does not move the count at which divergence stops, which stays at 40 for
CHF and COPD under both fits. The threshold is not an artifact of the
estimator, though what sets its location is not established here. One continuous variable and one outcome
were examined, Renal Failure and Hypertension were screened but not
analyzed, the pool excludes the short, high-mortality stays that carry no
first-day creatinine, and all results come from a single MIMIC-IV
snapshot.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}

The positive count governs every quantity measured here, and sample size
governs none of them. Divergence falls to zero between counts of forty
and eighty in all three comorbidities and both data sources; interquartile width falls
by 42--66\% over a matched increase in the count; CART's use of the
comorbidity is flat across a sixteen-fold change in $n$; and a logistic
fit to the same subsamples shows no consistent separation from its
permutation null under either lever. Four readings on the same replicates, so not four independent
confirmations, but two of them come from data the synthesizer never
touched. In that narrow sense the finding from Bayesian logistic
regression does extend to CART-based synthesis.

The wider claim does not survive contact with clinical effect sizes. The
simulation shows what recovery looks like when the target is large enough
to reach, and it is partial: at a true value of 3 the synthetic median
reaches 1.22. At practical subsample sizes neither arm recovers the
MIMIC-IV interactions at all:
even the narrowest cell spans nearly four times the quantity being
estimated, and with the count held at 20 the COPD synthetic median
carries the wrong sign at every sample size tested. Where the two can be compared, the synthetic
side is worse on both failure and spread.

For synthetic data evaluation, two things follow: establish
that the target effect is estimable in the source data before attributing
a discrepancy to the generator, and calibrate any variable-selection
diagnostic against a permutation null, since its null moves with class
balance from zero to 0.32 across the range tested here.
